# Low-Gas and Equipment Use

This note records how equipment and fuel constraints should shape recipe
selection.

## Core Constraint

The stovetop is gas and runs on gas bottles in a remote area. Gas is available,
but long continuous stovetop cooking should be avoided when another method can
produce an equal or better result.

## Preferred Long-Cook Methods

Use these freely for long elapsed cooking:

- slow cooker,
- fan-forced electric oven,
- microwave where suitable,
- Japanese rice cooker,
- air fryer for crisping or reheating.

Long unattended cooking is generally welcome when it does not involve an open
flame.

## Gas-Friendly Uses

Gas is well suited to:

- quick frying,
- browning,
- boiling,
- reheating,
- starting a soup, stew, or pulse dish before moving to retained heat or
  another appliance.

## Gas-Saving Pattern

A useful house pattern:

1. Bring a dish to the boil on gas.
2. Turn off or reduce the gas.
3. Let retained heat continue the cooking.
4. Repeat the cycle if needed.

This pattern is especially relevant for grains, pulses, soups, and stews when
the slow cooker or oven is not being used.

## Anti-Patterns

- Do not plan unattended open-flame cooking.
- Avoid recipes that require `2` to `3` hours of continuous stovetop simmering.
- A `30` minute simmer can be acceptable when there is a good reason, but
  consider slow-cooker, oven, or retained-heat alternatives first.

## Cookware Safety

- Stovetop cookware is stainless steel.
- Avoid Teflon and other nonstick-coated cookware because cockatiels are in the
  house and overheated nonstick fumes can be dangerous.
- Where a recipe assumes nonstick, adapt it to stainless steel, cast iron,
  glass, air fryer, oven, or another safe vessel.
