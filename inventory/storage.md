# Storage and Leftovers

This file records fridge, freezer, pantry-visibility, and leftover assumptions
for weekly planning.

## Fridge

- The household fridge is large, but much of it is used for food stocked for
  adult children, the user's wife, and the user's mother-in-law.
- Planning assumption: the user effectively has about one quarter of a large
  fridge available.
- Fridge space is not a severe constraint, but do not overbuy bulky
  perishables.
- Buy perishables in amounts likely to be consumed within a week.
- There is enough fridge space for a couple of large containers of leftovers.

## Freezer

- The user has one small freezer section for personal goods.
- Personal freezer space is enough for about a week or so of leftovers.
- The broader household freezer can store useful frozen base products such as
  frozen peas or mixed vegetables for longer periods.
- Use freezer space selectively for backup portions, frozen staples, and
  bread, not huge personal stockpiles.

## Leftover Tolerance

- Food kept in sealed fridge containers for up to `4` or `5` days is
  acceptable.
- Frozen meals are acceptable and useful.
- On cooking day, one portion is usually eaten fresh, but fridge-preserved and
  frozen leftovers are welcome.
- Planned leftovers are a feature of the workflow, not a compromise.

## Containers

- There are plenty of sealable plastic storage containers for the intended
  batch-cooking workflow.

## Pantry Visibility

- Pantry visibility has been a problem because the pantry is large.
- Items can be found physically, but are easy to forget when imagining new
  recipes.
- A major purpose of this repo is to prevent pantry amnesia and encourage use
  of the full breadth and diversity of the pantry and fridge.
