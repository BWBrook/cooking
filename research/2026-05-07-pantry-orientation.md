# Pantry Orientation and Repo Baseline

## Question

What kind of cooking notebook should this repository become, given the current
pantry, fridge staples, and the companion baking notebook?

## Synthesis

This repo should function as the general-cooking sibling of the baking
notebook. It should not be a generic recipe dump. Its main job is to translate
the current larder into interesting weekly cooking options, while clearly
separating what is already stocked from what should be bought on demand.

The current pantry is unusually strong in durable ingredients rather than fresh
perishables. That makes it especially well suited to pantry-led recipes built
around grains, pulses, canned fish, preserved condiments, sauces, and spices,
with fresh herbs, vegetables, meat, and citrus added deliberately for the week.

## Ingredient Model

The repo should track three layers:

- stock items normally available in pantry, fridge, freezer, or baking
  crossover,
- on-demand perishables bought for the chosen recipes,
- recipe-specific non-perishables bought only when committing to a dish or
  cuisine experiment.

This distinction is central. Fresh ingredients are welcome, but they should be
planned purchases rather than silently assumed stock.

## Strategic Implications

- The first recipes should probably be pantry-led but not ascetic: let the
  pantry provide structure, then buy a small number of fresh ingredients that
  make the dish vivid.
- Canned fish plus preserved lemon, capers, olives, herbs, and grains is a very
  promising early track.
- Lentil, split-pea, chickpea, and barley dishes should be recurring anchors.
- Weekly candidates should explicitly list stock ingredients and what must be
  bought.
- Fresh herbs are likely one of the highest-return on-demand purchases for this
  pantry.
