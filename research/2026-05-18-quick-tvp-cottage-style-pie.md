# Quick TVP Cottage-Style Pie

## Question

How should a small, robust, two-serving cottage/shepherd's-pie style dish be
adapted using TVP for mince and Deb instant mash for the potato topping?

## Short Answer

Treat this as a cottage-style TVP pie: a savoury mince-like filling under a
mashed potato top, but scaled down to two robust meals and designed for speed.
TVP works best if rehydrated in a strongly flavoured gravy base before
thickening. Deb works best as a slightly firmer-than-usual mash so it can sit
as a topping rather than collapse into the filling.

## Sources

- Britannica describes shepherd's pie as a British Isles dish built from
  vegetables and chopped meat in a casserole with mashed potatoes, and notes
  the lamb-versus-cottage-pie naming distinction.
- Bob's Red Mill describes TVP as a defatted soy-flour product that can be used
  as a meat substitute in recipes calling for ground beef or turkey. Its listed
  serving is `23 g`, with `80 kcal` and `12 g` protein.
- Woolworths' product page for Continental Deb Instant Mashed Potato records
  the Australian `350 g` pantry product, its ingredient/allergen notes, and a
  prepared serving size of `190 g`.
- Continental's Deb preparation page records the instant-mash method: boiling
  water into a bowl, stir in Deb, stand briefly, and stir again. It also gives
  a useful one-serving ratio of about `1/2 cup` Deb to `170 ml` boiling water.

## Adaptation Logic

The user asked for simple and relatively quick, with two explicit
substitutions:

- TVP instead of lamb mince,
- Deb instant mash instead of whole potatoes.

The core risk is blandness. TVP can feel mince-like, but it needs to hydrate in
seasoned liquid rather than plain water. The filling therefore uses stock
powder, Vegemite or Marmite, Worcestershire sauce, tomato paste, gravy powder,
onion powder, garlic powder, thyme, rosemary, mustard powder, and pepper.

For a vegetarian version, use vegetable stock and check whether the
Worcestershire sauce contains anchovies, or replace it with soy sauce.

The second risk is a loose or collapsing top. Deb is designed as an instant side
mash, but for a pie lid it should be prepared slightly firmer than normal.
Using roughly `1 cup` flakes and `300` to `340 ml` boiling water gives a two-
serve topping that can be fork-ruffled and baked.

## Open Questions for First Cook

- Is `80 g` dry TVP enough for two robust meals, or should the default move to
  `100 g`?
- Does `1 cup` Deb make the right topping thickness for the available small
  Pyrex/casserole dish?
- Is the base filling too salty with stock powder, gravy powder, Deb,
  Worcestershire, and Vegemite/Marmite?
- Should frozen mixed vegetables become a default on-demand add-in for this
  recipe?
