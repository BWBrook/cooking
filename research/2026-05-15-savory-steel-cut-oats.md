# Savoury Steel-Cut Oats

## Question

What is a filling, savoury, pantry-aware recipe that uses steel-cut oats as the
base and fits the household preference for low-gas cooking?

## Source Notes

| Source | Useful Pattern | Adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quaker, `Wild Mushroom Steel Cut Oat Risotto` | Uses steel-cut oats as a risotto-like grain with mushrooms, stock, herbs, acid, and optional dairy. | Keep the mushroom-oat dinner framing, but avoid the long stirred stovetop method and nonstick-pan assumption. |
| Tiger Corporation, `Savory Japanese Breakfast Oatmeal` | Cooks steel-cut oats with water and stock in a rice cooker, then tops with egg, scallions, soy sauce, and furikake. | Use the rice-cooker method as the main equipment path, with miso, tsuyu, nori, spring onion, and egg. |
| Institute of Culinary Education, `Savory Oats with Sauteed Mushrooms and Fried Eggs` | Treats oats, mushrooms, greens, egg, and cheese as a complete savoury meal. | Keep the mushrooms, greens, and egg logic, but shift the flavour base toward the existing Japanese pantry. |

## Synthesis

Steel-cut oats are a good savoury dinner base because they keep a chewy grain
identity while becoming creamy enough to carry umami sauces. The most
repo-compatible version is not a traditional stovetop risotto, because that
would require sustained open-flame gas use and regular stirring. A rice-cooker
base is a better fit: oats and liquid can cook mostly unattended, then miso can
be stirred in at the end for aroma and salt control.

The pantry already supports a Japanese-leaning version: red miso, kombu or
shiitake tsuyu, sesame oil, soy sauce, nori, eggs, and optional Sichuan or
Aleppo pepper. Mushrooms and spring onions are the main on-demand purchases.
Leafy greens are optional but useful if this is standing alone as dinner.

## Open Questions for First Cook

- Does `750 ml` liquid work for `160 g` steel-cut oats in the household rice
  cooker, or should the baseline move toward `850 ml`?
- Are egg ribbons in the hot oats satisfying enough, or is a separate jammy egg
  worth the extra step?
- Is `20 g` miso enough, or does the dish need closer to `30 g`?
- Are air-fried mushrooms good enough for this dish, or is a brief stainless
  pan saute noticeably better?
