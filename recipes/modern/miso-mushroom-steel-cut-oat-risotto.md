# Miso-Mushroom Steel-Cut Oat Risotto

## Goal

Make a filling savoury dinner from steel-cut oats: chewy, creamy, deeply
umami, and built around the rice cooker rather than a long stovetop simmer.

This is more "oat risotto" than breakfast porridge, but the method is
deliberately practical: rice cooker oats, roasted or air-fried mushrooms,
miso stirred in at the end, and egg used as the main protein anchor.

## Source Basis

This house version draws on three modern savoury-oat patterns:

- mushroom steel-cut oat risotto, where oats replace rice in a creamy mushroom
  dish,
- rice-cooker steel-cut oatmeal with Japanese savoury toppings,
- mushroom, greens, and egg-topped savoury oats as a complete meal.

See `research/2026-05-15-savory-steel-cut-oats.md` for the source note.

## Why It Fits This Pantry

- Stock strengths used: steel-cut oats, red miso, shiitake or kombu tsuyu,
  sesame oil, soy sauce, nori, eggs, black pepper, Sichuan peppercorns, and
  Aleppo pepper.
- On-demand ingredients needed: mushrooms and spring onions; leafy greens are
  useful but optional.
- Specialty items needed: none.
- Equipment path: rice cooker for the oats, air fryer or oven for mushrooms,
  and optional brief saucepan use for eggs.
- Active-attention fit: low. Most cooking is unattended and electric.
- Gas fit: excellent if the egg is stirred into the hot oats or cooked by a
  brief boil; no long gas simmer.

## Ingredients

### Stock Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steel-cut oats | 160 g | About `1 cup`; main grain base. |
| Water | 750 ml | Start here; add more after cooking if the oats are too stiff. |
| Shiitake or kombu tsuyu | 1 tbsp | Optional but useful. If using stock powder instead, reduce or skip this. |
| Red miso paste | 20 to 30 g | Stir in at the end as a slurry. Start lower for salt control. |
| Sesame oil | 2 tsp | Half for mushrooms, half to finish. |
| Eggs | 2 large | Stir in as egg ribbons, or cook separately as jammy/fried eggs. |
| Nori | 1 sheet | Tear or snip over the bowls. |
| Soy sauce | 1 to 2 tsp, optional | Use at the table only if needed; miso and tsuyu are already salty. |
| Black pepper | to taste | Default pepper finish. |
| Sichuan peppercorns | tiny pinch, optional | Toast and crush; excellent with mushrooms if used lightly. |
| Aleppo pepper | pinch, optional | Warm chilli finish without overwhelming the miso. |
| Sesame seeds | 1 to 2 tsp, optional | Garnish. |

### On-Demand Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mushrooms | 250 to 300 g | Swiss brown, button, oyster, shiitake, or a mix. Slice thickly. |
| Spring onions | 2 | Whites can cook with mushrooms; greens for garnish. |
| Leafy greens | 80 to 120 g, optional | Spinach, kale, silverbeet, or similar. Add at the end to wilt. |
| Fresh ginger | 5 to 10 g, optional | Grate into the oats or mushrooms if bought for the week. |

### Optional Protein or Bulk Additions

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canned salmon or tuna | 100 to 150 g drained | Fold through one bowl or the whole batch. |
| Firm tofu | 150 to 200 g | Cube and air fry with the mushrooms. |
| Extra egg | 1 | Use if this is replacing a heavy dinner. |
| Frozen peas | 80 to 100 g | Add near the end of the rice-cooker cycle or microwave separately. |

## Method

1. Put the steel-cut oats, water, and optional tsuyu into the rice cooker.
   Stir once.
2. Cook on the `oatmeal`, `porridge`, or `brown rice` setting. For the first
   trial, check once early and once near the end in case this rice cooker
   foams with oats.
3. While the oats cook, toss the mushrooms with `1 tsp` sesame oil. Add the
   sliced spring onion whites if using.
4. Air fry the mushrooms at `180 C` for about `8` to `12` minutes, shaking
   once, until browned and reduced. Alternatively roast them on a tray in the
   electric oven at `200 C` for about `15` to `20` minutes.
5. If using leafy greens, wash and chop them while the mushrooms cook.
6. When the oats are tender, spoon a little hot oat liquid into a cup and mix
   it with the miso to make a smooth slurry.
7. Stir the miso slurry back into the oats. If the mixture is too thick, add
   `50` to `150 ml` hot water and stir again.
8. Stir in the leafy greens, if using. Close the rice cooker for `2` to `3`
   minutes to wilt them.
9. For the lowest-effort egg path, beat the eggs in a cup, set the rice cooker
   to `keep warm`, drizzle the eggs slowly into the hot oats while stirring,
   then close the lid for `2` minutes. The result should be soft egg ribbons,
   not scrambled chunks.
10. Fold through most of the mushrooms, keeping some for the top.
11. Taste before adding soy sauce. The miso and tsuyu may already be enough.
12. Serve in bowls topped with the remaining mushrooms, spring onion greens,
    torn nori, the remaining `1 tsp` sesame oil, sesame seeds, black pepper,
    and optional Aleppo or a tiny pinch of crushed Sichuan peppercorn.

## Separate Egg Option

If you want the visual and texture of an egg on top, skip step `9` and cook
the eggs separately:

1. Bring a small saucepan of water to a boil.
2. Add eggs and cook `7` minutes for jammy yolks or `9` minutes for firmer
   eggs.
3. Cool briefly, peel, halve, and place on the bowls.

This uses a little gas but only briefly. Fried eggs also work, but stainless
steel frying needs good preheating and enough fat.

## Timing

- Prep time: `10` to `15` minutes.
- Active time: about `15` minutes.
- Inactive/elapsed time: rice-cooker cycle plus `8` to `20` minutes for
  mushrooms, depending on air fryer or oven.
- Make-ahead options: the oat base reheats well; add nori, spring onions, and
  sesame oil fresh.

## Equipment and Fuel Notes

- Primary equipment: Japanese rice cooker, air fryer or electric oven.
- Gas use: none if using egg ribbons; brief saucepan use if making jammy eggs.
- Unattended cooking: rice cooker and air fryer/oven do most of the work.
- Nonstick dependence: none.
- Reheating method: microwave individual portions with a splash of water.

## Batch and Nutrition Notes

- Target meals: `2` generous dinners, or `3` lighter meals with extra greens
  or another side.
- Finished batch weight: weigh after cooking for Garmin tracking.
- Target portion weight: half the finished batch for a main meal.
- Garmin entry strategy: enter oats by dry weight, eggs by count or weight,
  mushrooms by raw weight, sesame oil by grams or teaspoons, miso by grams,
  and tsuyu/soy separately for sodium awareness.
- Rough calorie expectation: about `900` to `1,000 kcal` for the whole batch
  before optional fish, tofu, or extra egg; roughly `450` to `500 kcal` per
  half batch.
- Sodium note: miso, tsuyu, soy sauce, stock powders, and nori can stack up
  quickly. Start with miso plus tsuyu, then add soy sauce only at the table if
  the bowl actually needs it.

## Result Targets

- Texture: creamy and spoonable, with chewy oat grains still distinct.
- Flavor: mushroom-forward, miso-savoury, gently nutty from sesame, with nori
  and spring onion freshness.
- Appearance: dark mushrooms over pale-gold oats with green onion and nori.
- Leftovers: should keep `3` to `4` days refrigerated; loosen with water when
  reheating.

## Variations

- **More Japanese bowl:** use kombu tsuyu, nori, sesame, spring onion, and a
  jammy egg; keep black pepper restrained.
- **More mushroom depth:** add a few chopped dried shiitake if available,
  soaking them first and using the soaking liquid as part of the cooking
  liquid.
- **More heat:** finish with Aleppo pepper, chilli oil, or a tiny amount of
  crushed Sichuan peppercorn.
- **More protein:** add canned salmon, canned tuna, tofu, or a third egg.
- **More green:** add spinach, kale, silverbeet, peas, or cabbage.
- **More Western risotto-like:** skip tsuyu and nori, use vegetable or chicken
  stock, finish with butter and Parmesan if bought for the week.
