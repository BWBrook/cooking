# Medieval Herbolace / Erbolate Herb Egg Cake

## Goal

Make a small medieval-style herb-and-egg bake: intensely green, savoury,
lightly buttered, and substantial enough for one dinner or two lighter meals.

## Historical Basis

This recipe is a modern adaptation of the `Forme of Cury` erbolate/herbolade
family: chopped herbs mixed with eggs, added to butter in a dish, baked, and
served.

The working name `Herbolace` is kept because it is memorable and close to the
medieval herb-dish family, but the egg-cake source is more precisely the
`Erbolate` / `Herbolade` tradition rather than Pegge's broth-like `Hebolace`
entry.

See `research/2026-05-07-medieval-herbolace-erbolate.md` for the source note.

## Why It Fits This Pantry

- Stock strengths used: free-range eggs, butter, dried mint, sage, thyme,
  chives, parsley flakes, black pepper, nutmeg or ginger.
- On-demand ingredients needed: fresh parsley plus one or two fresh green
  herbs or alliums.
- Specialty items needed: none.
- Equipment path: buttered Pyrex dish or cast-iron skillet in the electric
  oven.
- Active-attention fit: low. Chopping is the main work; baking is unattended.

## Ingredients

### Stock Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eggs | 6 large | The household has laying hens, so this is a stock-strength ingredient. |
| Butter | 10 to 15 g | For the dish and flavour. |
| Dried sage | 1/4 tsp | Optional but historically apt; keep restrained. |
| Dried thyme or marjoram | 1/2 tsp | Optional support if fresh herb mix is simple. |
| Dried mint | 1/2 tsp | Use if fresh mint is not bought. |
| Black pepper | 1/4 tsp | Or more to taste. |
| Nutmeg or ground ginger | tiny pinch | Optional medieval-ish warmth; do not overdo it. |
| Salt | small pinch | Optional; keep light. |

## Method

1. Heat the oven to `170 C` fan-forced.
2. Choose a small Pyrex dish or the cast-iron skillet. Butter it generously.
3. Wash and dry the fresh herbs. Chop them finely. Aim for a visibly green egg
   mixture, not a few decorative flecks.
4. Beat the eggs in a bowl until combined but not foamy.
5. Stir in the fresh herbs, any dried herbs, pepper, optional nutmeg or ginger,
   and a small pinch of salt.
6. Pour the mixture into the buttered dish.
7. Bake for `18` to `25` minutes, until just set in the centre. Start checking
   early; overbaked egg will become rubbery.
8. Rest `5` minutes before cutting.
9. Serve warm with toast/sippets, or with a small yogurt-preserved lemon side
   if you want a sharper modern plate.

## Timing

- Prep time: `10` to `15` minutes.
- Active time: about `15` minutes.
- Inactive/elapsed time: `18` to `25` minutes baking, plus `5` minutes resting.
- Make-ahead options: best fresh, but leftovers should reheat gently or eat
  well cold.

## Equipment and Fuel Notes

- Primary equipment: Pyrex dish or cast-iron skillet, electric oven.
- Gas use: none.
- Unattended cooking: oven baking is mostly unattended after prep.
- Reheating method: microwave gently at lower power, or eat cold/room

## Batch and Nutrition Notes

- Target meals: `1` generous dinner or `2` lighter meals.
- Finished batch weight: weigh after baking if tracking closely.
- Target portion weight: half the cake if serving as `2` portions.
- Garmin entry strategy: enter eggs by count/weight, butter by grams, herbs as
  low-calorie additions, and any bread/yogurt/preserved lemon/caper side
  separately.
- Rough calorie expectation: the egg cake alone is likely around `500` to
  `600 kcal` for the full six-egg batch, depending on egg size and butter
  amount, before bread or sides.

## Result Targets

- Texture: tender set egg, closer to a shallow frittata than a fluffy omelette.
- Flavor: green, herbal, lightly buttery, peppery, and savoury.
- Appearance: speckled or deeply green depending on herb load.
- Leftovers: acceptable for one follow-up meal, but this is not a major batch
  dish.

## Variations

- **Pantry-only fallback:** use dried parsley, mint, chives, sage, and thyme,
  but expect a less vivid result.
- **More courtly spice:** add a tiny pinch of ginger and nutmeg.
- **More modern bright plate:** serve with Greek yogurt, preserved lemon, and
  capers.
- **More substantial meal:** serve with toast/sippets and a small lentil,
  barley, or bean side.
