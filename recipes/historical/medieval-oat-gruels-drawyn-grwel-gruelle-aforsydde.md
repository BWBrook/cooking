# Medieval Oat Gruels - Drawyn Grwel and Gruelle a-forsydde

## Goal

Make a pair of late-medieval English savoury oat gruels from Harleian MS 279:
a pork-and-saffron `Gruelle a-forsydde` and a beef, parsley, and sage
`Drawyn grwel`.

These are not sweet breakfast porridges. They are thin, strained, meat-based
pottages: more like a smooth, savoury, oat-thickened soup than a bowl of modern
oatmeal.

For the first cook, choose one variant rather than buying both meats. The
pork-saffron version is the simpler first trial because it does not require a
decision about the blood-colour element in `Drawyn grwel`.

## Historical Basis

Both recipes appear in the `Potage Dyvers` section of Harleian MS 279, printed
in Thomas Austin's `Two Fifteenth-Century Cookery-Books`.

`Gruelle a-forcyd`, often modernised as `Gruelle a-forsydde`, is an oatmeal
and pork gruel. The source directs the cook to grind oatmeal small, boil it
well with pork, pound the cooked pork, strain the mixture, salt it, colour it
with saffron, and serve it runny.

`Drawyn grwel` is a beef-and-oat gruel with parsley and sage. The source uses
lean beef, oatmeal, parsley, sage, salt, and blood for a brown colour. The
modern baseline below omits fresh blood for availability and food-safety
reasons, with black pudding offered only as an optional darkening nod.

See `research/2026-05-15-medieval-oat-gruels.md` for the source note.

## Which Oats?

Use plain instant oats for the first trial.

The medieval ingredient is oatmeal ground small and then strained. Plain
instant oats behave much more like fine oat meal than intact steel-cut oats:
they hydrate quickly, blend smooth, and pass through a sieve without a long
stovetop cook.

Steel-cut oats can be used only if treated first:

- pulse them in the blender into a coarse meal before cooking, or
- cook them very soft, then blend and strain.

For this recipe, steel-cut oats are historically interesting but practically
less faithful to the required smooth, runny finish.

## Why It Fits This Pantry

- Stock strengths used: instant oats, sage, parsley flakes, imitation saffron,
  salt, blender, slow cooker, and sieve.
- On-demand ingredients needed: pork or lean beef; fresh parsley if making the
  beef-herb version with a brighter flavour.
- Specialty items needed: none for the baseline.
- Optional specialty items: real saffron or black pudding.
- Equipment path: slow cooker for meat/broth, blender and sieve for the drawn
  texture, microwave or brief saucepan finish.
- Active-attention fit: moderate but modular.
- Gas fit: good. The long meat cook is electric; any stovetop finish is brief
  and can be replaced with microwave heating.

## Variant 1: Gruelle a-forsydde - Pork and Saffron Oat Gruel

This is the more direct pantry-comfort version: pork broth, finely worked pork,
oats, salt, and saffron colour.

### Ingredients

#### Stock Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plain instant oats | 80 g | Do not use sweetened/flavoured sachets. |
| Water | 1.2 L | For cooking the pork and making the gruel. |
| Imitation yellow saffron | small pinch to 1/8 tsp | Colour note. Use real saffron if bought. |
| Salt | to taste | Add late. Pork may contribute salt if cured. |

#### On-Demand Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pork hock, pork ribs, pork neck, or pork shoulder chop | 300 to 450 g bone-in if possible | Bone and skin are source-adjacent; boneless pork shoulder also works. |

#### Optional Additions

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| White pepper or black pepper | tiny pinch | Not in the source, but acceptable if the first bowl tastes flat. |
| Ginger | tiny pinch | Courtly spice option, not part of the baseline. |

### Method

1. Put the pork and water into the slow cooker.
2. Cook on `LOW` for `6` to `8` hours, or on `HIGH` for `3` to `4` hours,
   until the pork is very tender.
3. Lift out the pork. Strain the broth and skim excess fat if needed.
4. Pick meat from bone and skin. Discard bone. Keep a little soft skin only if
   you want a richer, more gelatinous gruel.
5. Chop the meat finely, then pulse it briefly in the blender with a ladle of
   broth. A future mortar and pestle would be a more source-like tool here, but
   the blender is the practical current option.
6. Put `850 ml` of pork broth into a large microwave-safe bowl or small
   saucepan. Keep extra broth nearby for thinning.
7. Whisk in the instant oats.
8. Microwave in short bursts, stirring often, or heat gently in a saucepan for
   `5` to `8` minutes, until the oats are fully hydrated.
9. Stir in the worked pork and saffron colour.
10. Blend briefly, then pass through a sieve for the drawn medieval texture.
    Press with a spoon to capture as much smooth body as practical.
11. Return the strained gruel to heat for `2` to `3` minutes.
12. Salt to taste.
13. Serve runny in bowls. If it stands up like porridge, thin it with hot broth
    or water.

## Variant 2: Drawyn Grwel - Beef, Parsley, and Sage Oat Gruel

This is the darker, more herbaceous version. The original uses blood to brown
the gruel; this house version treats that as optional rather than a baseline.

### Ingredients

#### Stock Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plain instant oats | 80 g | Fine oat base for a strained gruel. |
| Water | 1.2 L | Original logic is water plus lean beef. |
| Dried sage | 1/4 tsp | Strong; keep restrained. |
| Dried parsley flakes | 1 tsp | Use only if fresh parsley is not bought. |
| Salt | to taste | Add late. |

#### On-Demand Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lean beef | 250 to 350 g | Gravy beef, shin, skirt, or another stewing cut. |
| Fresh flat-leaf parsley | 15 to 25 g, optional | Better than dried parsley if available. |

#### Optional Darkening Nod

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Black pudding or blood sausage | 40 to 80 g | Optional modern substitute for the blood-colour idea. Not needed for the first trial. |

### Method

1. Put the beef and water into the slow cooker.
2. Cook on `LOW` for `6` to `8` hours, or on `HIGH` for `3` to `4` hours,
   until the beef is very tender.
3. Lift out the beef. Strain the broth and skim excess fat if needed.
4. Shred or chop the beef finely.
5. Put `850 ml` of beef broth into a large microwave-safe bowl or small
   saucepan. Keep extra broth nearby for thinning.
6. Whisk in the instant oats.
7. Microwave in short bursts, stirring often, or heat gently in a saucepan for
   `5` to `8` minutes, until the oats are fully hydrated.
8. Stir in the shredded beef, sage, and parsley.
9. If using black pudding, crumble it in now and heat until fully dispersed.
10. Blend briefly, then pass through a sieve for the drawn texture.
11. Return the strained gruel to heat for `2` to `3` minutes.
12. Salt to taste.
13. Serve runny in bowls. Add hot broth or water if it thickens while standing.

## Less-Strained Meal Version

For a more filling modern dinner, strain only half the gruel and return the
pressed oat-meat solids to the bowl. This is less source-like but much more
substantial and easier to track nutritionally.

Another good compromise is to make the source-like smooth gruel, then reserve
a small amount of shredded pork or beef as a garnish. Label that clearly in the
cook log as a modern serving choice.

## Timing

- Prep time: `10` to `20` minutes.
- Active time: about `25` to `35` minutes, mostly after the meat is cooked.
- Inactive/elapsed time: `3` to `8` hours for the slow cooker.
- Make-ahead options: cook the meat and broth a day ahead, then finish the
  gruel quickly the next day.

## Equipment and Fuel Notes

- Primary equipment: slow cooker, blender, sieve, large bowl or small saucepan.
- Gas use: none if finishing in the microwave; about `5` to `10` minutes if
  finishing in a saucepan.
- Unattended cooking: excellent for the meat/broth stage.
- Nonstick dependence: none.
- Reheating method: microwave with extra water or broth; these thicken as they
  stand.

## Batch and Nutrition Notes

- Target meals: `2` generous bowls or `3` smaller bowls per variant.
- Finished batch weight: weigh after straining and final thinning.
- Target portion weight: half the batch for a main meal; one third for a
  smaller bowl with bread or another side.
- Garmin entry strategy: enter oats by dry weight. Enter pork or beef by raw
  weight if using all solids; if straining heavily and discarding solids, weigh
  the discarded solids and note that the calorie estimate is approximate.
- For the most Garmin-friendly version, keep and eat the pressed solids rather
  than discarding them.
- Salt note: the pork version may need less salt if using cured or salted pork.

## Result Targets

- Texture: runny, smooth, pourable from a ladle, but with enough oat body to
  feel nourishing.
- `Gruelle a-forsydde` flavour: mild pork broth, oat body, saffron colour,
  simple salt finish.
- `Drawyn grwel` flavour: beef broth, sage, parsley, darker savoury depth.
- Appearance: pale gold for the pork-saffron version; beige-brown to darker
  brown for the beef-herb version.
- Leftovers: best within `3` days refrigerated; loosen aggressively when
  reheating.

## Variations

- **Steel-cut experiment:** pulse `80 g` steel-cut oats in the blender before
  cooking, then expect a longer simmer and a coarser final texture.
- **More source-like pork version:** choose pork with skin and bone, work the
  meat very fine, strain thoroughly, and keep the seasoning minimal.
- **More source-like beef version:** use lean beef, fresh parsley, sage, and a
  safe black-pudding darkening note if desired.
- **More practical dinner:** leave it less strained and serve with toasted
  house bread from the baking repo.
- **Sickbed-restorative version:** strain fully, thin more heavily, and serve
  as a savoury drinking gruel.
