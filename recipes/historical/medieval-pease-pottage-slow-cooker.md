# Medieval-Style Pease Pottage for the Slow Cooker

## Goal

Make a thick, comforting, historically anchored English pease pottage: mostly
green split peas, onion, herbs, and a modest bacon accent, adapted for the large
slow cooker and for practical weekly leftovers.

## Historical Basis

This is a modern home-kitchen adaptation, not a literal reconstruction.

The late-fourteenth-century `Forme of Cury` gives a pea puree tradition:
peas cooked until they burst, then worked smooth and finished with onions, oil,
saffron, salt, and a little sugar. A related pea-and-herb recipe in the same
collection supports the use of herbs, onions, saffron, and sweet spice.

The bacon-and-mint direction comes from later English pease-pottage tradition,
especially Robert May's seventeenth-century recipes using old peas with bacon,
mint or sweet herbs, butter, and pepper.

See `research/2026-05-07-medieval-pease-pottage.md` for the source note.

## Why It Fits This Pantry

- Stock strengths used: green split peas, dried mint, thyme, sage, bay, black
  pepper, imitation yellow saffron, butter or olive oil.
- On-demand ingredients needed: brown onion and bacon; fresh mint or parsley if
  wanted.
- Specialty items needed: none.
- Equipment path: large slow cooker, with optional brief stainless-pan browning
  or air-fryer bacon crisping.
- Active-attention fit: low. Prep is modular, then the slow cooker does the
  long work without extended gas use.

## Ingredients

### Stock Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dry green split peas | 500 g | Rinse and pick over. No soak required, but a 2 to 4 hour soak is fine. |
| Water or unsalted stock | 1.8 L to start | Use water for the cleanest historical baseline; add more later if too thick. |
| Dried mint | 1 tsp | Increase later if mint reads too faintly. |
| Dried thyme | 1 tsp | Part of the sweet-herb profile. |
| Dried sage | 1/4 to 1/2 tsp | Keep restrained. |
| Bay leaves | 2 | Remove before serving. |
| Black pepper | 1/2 tsp, plus more to finish | Historically plausible and useful with peas. |
| Imitation yellow saffron | small pinch or 1/8 tsp | Optional colour note; not a flavour driver. |
| White sugar | 1 tsp | Optional `Forme of Cury` nod; omit if unwanted. |
| Butter | 10 to 20 g | Stir in at the end, Robert May-style. Use olive oil if preferred. |
| Salt | to taste at the end | Start low because bacon may be salty. |

### On-Demand Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brown onion | 1 large, about 200 g | Finely chopped. |
| Bacon | 100 to 150 g | Smoked streaky bacon, middle bacon, or speck. Use as accent, not bulk. |
| Fresh mint or flat-leaf parsley | optional handful | Best added fresh at serving if bought for the week. |

### Optional or Specialty Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Powder douce-style finish | tiny pinch | Optional courtly variant: mostly cinnamon with a little ginger. Use cautiously. |
| Toasted bread or sippets | as desired | Historically apt serving partner; use house bread from the baking repo if available. |

## Method

1. Rinse the split peas well and pick out any debris.
2. Put the peas into the slow cooker with `1.8 L` water or unsalted stock.
3. Add the chopped onion, dried mint, thyme, sage, bay leaves, pepper,
   imitation saffron if using, and the optional sugar.
4. Add the bacon in one of these ways:
   - lowest-fuss: add diced bacon directly to the slow cooker,
   - better flavour, little gas: brown diced bacon and onion briefly in a
     stainless pan for `5` to `8` minutes, then scrape everything into the slow
     cooker,
   - crisp garnish: cook bacon separately in the air fryer and reserve some or
     all for serving.
5. Cook on `LOW` for `7` to `9` hours, or on `HIGH` for `4` to `5` hours,
   until the peas have collapsed and the pottage is thick.
6. Stir hard with a spoon or whisk to break up the peas. For a smoother pottage,
   transfer a portion carefully to the blender, blend, and return it to the
   slow cooker. A future stick blender would make this easier.
7. If too thick, add hot water in `100 ml` increments. If too thin, cook
   uncovered on `HIGH` for `20` to `40` minutes, stirring occasionally.
8. Remove the bay leaves.
9. Stir in the butter. Taste, then add salt only as needed.
10. Finish with more black pepper and, if available, fresh mint or parsley.
11. Serve in bowls, optionally with toast or sippets and a small crisp bacon
    garnish.

## Timing

- Prep time: `15` to `25` minutes, depending on whether bacon/onion are browned.
- Active time: about `20` minutes total.
- Inactive/elapsed time: `4` to `9` hours depending on slow-cooker setting.
- Make-ahead options: excellent. Improves after resting and reheats well.

## Equipment and Fuel Notes

- Primary equipment: large `5.5` to `6 L` slow cooker.
- Gas use: none if bacon is added directly or cooked in the air fryer; `5` to
  `8` minutes if browning bacon/onion in a stainless pan.
- Unattended cooking: good fit; no open flame during the long cook.
- Reheating method: microwave individual portions or reheat gently with a
  splash of water.

## Result Targets

- Texture: thick, spoonable, mostly smooth but not baby-food smooth.
- Flavor: earthy peas, sweet onion, gentle herbs, pepper, and a savoury bacon
  undertone.
- Appearance: green-brown rustic pottage, lightly yellowed if saffron colour is
  used.
- Leftovers: should hold well for `4` to `5` days refrigerated and freeze well.

## Variations

- **Lenten-ish no-bacon version:** omit bacon, use olive oil, increase herbs,
  and consider a little mushroom powder or miso only if historical strictness is
  not the aim.
- **More May-style:** increase mint and butter, serve with thin toast/sippets.
- **More courtly `Forme of Cury` style:** use oil rather than bacon, keep
  saffron colour, include the tiny sugar note, and finish with a very cautious
  pinch of cinnamon-ginger powder.
- **Smokier pantry version:** use a little smoked paprika only if moving away
  from the historical profile.
