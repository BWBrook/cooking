## Equipment

### 1. Stovetop

Question: What stovetop do you have: induction, gas, ceramic, coil, or
something else?

Answer:

- Gas stovetop.
- Also volunteered at this point: air fryer, microwave, and slow cooker are on
  hand and likely to matter in follow-up equipment questions.

### 2. Ovens and Countertop Appliances Used for Cooking

Question: What ovens or countertop appliances do you actually use for cooking,
not just baking?

Answer:

- Fan-forced electric oven used for general baking and grilling.
- Gas burners used for frying and boiling with saucepans, and for Dutch-oven
  work.
- Large slow cooker is used regularly; estimated capacity is about `5.5` to
  `6 L`, suitable for larger meals.
- Cast-iron skillet is used when high heat capacity is useful.
- Important operating preference: preserve bottled gas where possible. Long
  continuous stovetop cooking should be avoided when a lower-gas method can
  work.
- Common gas-saving habit: bring a dish to the boil, turn off or reduce heat,
  let it stand and cook in retained heat, then repeat the cycle if needed.

Question: What saucepans, stockpots, frying pans, roasting trays, casserole

### 5. Most-Used Vessels

Question: What are your most-used cooking vessels in ordinary weeknight

## Storage

Question: How much fridge space can a weekly plan assume?

Answer:

- A lot of fridge space is used for food stocked for adult children, wife, and
  mother-in-law.
- Planning assumption: user effectively has about one quarter of a large
  fridge available.
- Do not overbuy bulky perishables.
- Buy the right amount of perishables for consumption within a week.

### 11. Containers

## Cooking Habits

### 13. Nights Per Week Cooking

Question: How many nights per week do you realistically want to cook?

Answer:

- Realistic target: about two nights per week cooking a primary recipe.
- User typically works from home on Tuesdays and Thursdays, making those good
  days for recipes that can be managed by going back and forth to the kitchen.
- Slow-cooker recipes and other low-attention/unattended methods are especially
  useful for work-from-home days.
- Weekends allow more exploratory and hands-on cooking.
- Bread is typically baked on weekends, then leftover bread is frozen for use
  during the week.
- Likely cadence: bake on one weekend day, cook a primary recipe on another
  weekend day, then cook again on Tuesday or Thursday if required.

### 14. Recipe Yield

Question: How many meals should a normal recipe make for you?

Answer:

- Normal target: `3` to `5` meals including the meal eaten fresh on cooking
  day.
- Simpler dishes can be cooked as smaller batches for about `1` to `2` meals.
- Some very quick/simple recipes may be single-meal with no leftovers.
- Some days may be "bits and pieces" rather than a planned recipe.
- Useful batch-size tiers:
  - single meal: eat once, no leftovers expected,
  - small batch: `1` to `2` days,
  - normal batch: `3` to `5` meals,
  - slow-cooker batch: often largest, potentially `5` to `6` meals.
- Slow cooker is the best tool for the largest batches because of its large
  volume; freezing some leftovers is acceptable and expected.
- For slow-cooker meals, it is reasonable to make substantial use of the
  estimated `6 L` capacity.

### 15. Planned Leftovers vs Variety

Question: Do you like planned leftovers, or do you prefer variety every meal?

Answer:

- Planned leftovers are good.
- User can happily eat the same meal three nights in a row.
- If two recipes with leftovers are available, user may alternate between them.
- No need for a different meal every night.
- Preferred pattern: two main recipes cooked over a week and switched between,
  with perhaps one day off for takeaway, leftovers from others, or bits and
  pieces.

### 16. Comfortable Weeknight Cooking Time

Question: What is a comfortable weeknight cooking time: 15, 30, 45, 60+
minutes?

Answer:

- Direct elapsed cooking time is not the main constraint.
- Weekday cooking often means daytime work-from-home cooking: start a process,
  return to the home office, then come back to the kitchen as needed so dinner
  is ready by evening.
- Weekend cooking can happen in the afternoon or early evening.
- Cooking time matters mainly if it requires constant attention.
- If forced to stay constantly in the kitchen, about `60` minutes in one
  session is the practical maximum.

### 17. Long Unattended Cooking

Question: Are you happy with long unattended cooking, such as simmering,
roasting, slow cooking, or soaking?

Answer:

- Yes. Long unattended cooking is a feature rather than a bug and is generally
  a preferred mode.
- Exception: stovetop gas. User does not want to leave an open flame
  unattended and does not want long periods of gas use.
- Gas is preferred for quicker frying, reheating, or bringing something to a
  boil before turning off the gas and letting retained heat work.
- A recipe needing about a `30` minute simmer can be acceptable when justified.
- A `2` to `3` hour stovetop gas simmer is highly undesirable and should be
  treated as an anti-pattern.

### 18. Prep Intensity

Question: Do you enjoy prep-heavy cooking, or should chopping and fiddly prep
be minimized?

Answer:

- Heavy prep is acceptable.
- User is a slow-food person at heart.
- Prep can happily be broken into modules rather than done all at once.

### 28. Nutrition Estimates

Answer:

- User maintains a strict food-intake diary using the Garmin app calorie
  tracker.
- For bulk meals, the most useful support is approximate gram weights of all
  main ingredients and divided portions, so portions are easy to enter into the
  app.
- Garmin app can auto-load associated calories once foods/weights are entered.
- Current dated context as of `2026-05-07`: user weighs about `70 kg`, is on a
  calorie deficit until reaching goal weight of `65 kg`, then plans to eat to
  maintenance.
- Estimated TDEE: about `2250 kcal`.

## Workflow

### 33. Weekly Plan Shape

Question: Do you want weekly plans built around one main dish, two mains, or a
mix of mains plus components?

Answer:

- Default weekly plan should be built around two mains.
- Add support components as required.

### 34. Candidate Recipes vs House Recipes

Question: Should the repo track "candidate recipes I might cook" separately
from "house recipes I have cooked and trust"?

Answer:

- Yes.
- Separate exploratory or possible-future-use recipes from trusted repeat
  house recipes.
- Some recipes will become regular favourites because user loves them.
- Other recipes may be worth preserving as exploratory or historical-interest
  candidates, like a Max Miller highlight, even if not repeated often.

### 35. Cook Log Detail

Question: When you cook something, how detailed do you want the log to be:
quick judgement, structured notes, or full recipe revision evidence?

Answer:

- Default should be quick judgement.
- If user wants to add further notes, a follow-up voice-mode debrief can be
  converted into a more coherent structured log.
- Planning implication: keep routine logs low-friction, with optional deeper
  evidence when a recipe deserves revision.

### 36. Standing Priorities File

Question: Would you like a standing "current priorities" file that tells Codex
what to suggest next?

Answer:

- Yes.
- A standing priorities file would be useful as a ranked list.
- It should not be treated as a canonical ratchet or rigid backlog.
- Purpose: capture "what is likely a good thing to try next" based on current
  pantry, interests, habits, and constraints.
